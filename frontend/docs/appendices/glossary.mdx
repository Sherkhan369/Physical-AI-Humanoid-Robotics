# Glossary

This glossary provides definitions for key terms and concepts used throughout "Physical AI: Bridging the Digital and Real Worlds through Robotics." Understanding these terms is essential for grasping the foundational and advanced topics discussed in the textbook.

---

**Action Space**
:   The set of all possible actions an agent can take in an environment. In robotics, this could be joint torques, velocities, or high-level commands.

**Actuator**
:   A component of a machine that is responsible for moving or controlling a mechanism or system. In robotics, actuators convert energy (electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic) into motion.

**Agent**
:   An entity that perceives its environment through sensors and acts upon that environment through actuators.

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)**
:   The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems. These processes include learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and decision-making.

**Augmented Reality (AR)**
:   An interactive experience of a real-world environment where the objects that reside in the real world are enhanced by computer-generated perceptual information.

**Autonomy**
:   The ability of a robot or system to perform tasks without continuous human guidance.

**Behavior Tree**
:   A mathematical model of plan execution used in computer science, robotics, and video games. They describe switching between a finite set of tasks in a modular fashion.

**Camera Sensor**
:   A sensor that captures visual information from the environment, often used for object detection, recognition, and navigation.

**Center of Mass (CoM)**
:   The unique point where the weighted relative position of the distributed mass sums to zero. Crucial for stability and balance control in humanoid robots.

**Collision Detection**
:   The algorithmic process of detecting when two or more objects in a simulation or real environment are overlapping or intersecting.

**Computer Vision (CV)**
:   A field of artificial intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world. Using digital images from cameras and videos and deep learning models, machines can accurately identify and classify objects and then react to what they “see.”

**CUDA**
:   (Compute Unified Device Architecture) A parallel computing platform and application programming interface (API) model created by NVIDIA. It allows software developers and software engineers to use a CUDA-enabled graphics processing unit (GPU) for general purpose processing.

**Digital Twin**
:   A virtual representation of a physical object or system across its lifecycle, updated from real-time data, and used for simulation, integration, testing, and monitoring.

**Distributed Control System (DCS)**
:   A control system for a process or plant, in which control elements are not central in location but distributed throughout the system with a central supervisory control.

**Dynamics**
:   The study of forces and their effect on motion. In robotics, it involves calculating how forces and torques influence a robot's movement.

**End-Effector**
:   The device or tool at the end of a robotic arm, designed to interact with the environment (e.g., gripper, welding torch, camera).

**Forward Kinematics (FK)**
:   The process of calculating the position and orientation of the end-effector of a robotic arm given the joint angles and link lengths.

**Force Sensor**
:   A sensor that measures forces (push or pull) applied to it. Used in robotics for tactile feedback, gripping, and balance control.

**Gazebo**
:   An open-source 3D robot simulator that accurately and efficiently simulates populations of robots in complex indoor and outdoor environments.

**Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)**
:   The study of interactions between humans and robots. It is a multidisciplinary field with contributions from human–computer interaction, artificial intelligence, robotics, natural language understanding, and social sciences.

**Inverse Kinematics (IK)**
:   The process of calculating the joint angles required to achieve a desired position and orientation for a robot's end-effector. Essential for goal-oriented robot movement.

**Isaac Gym**
:   NVIDIA's platform for high-performance GPU-accelerated reinforcement learning research, enabling massive parallelization of simulations.

**Isaac ROS**
:   NVIDIA's collection of GPU-accelerated packages for ROS 2, providing high-performance perception and processing capabilities for robotics applications.

**Isaac Sim**
:   NVIDIA's scalable robotics simulation platform and tools for developing, testing, and managing AI-based robots, built on NVIDIA Omniverse.

**Kinematics**
:   The study of motion without considering the forces that cause it. In robotics, it describes the geometry of robot motion.

**LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)**
:   A remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure ranges (variable distances) to the Earth. Commonly used in robotics for mapping and navigation.

**Machine Learning (ML)**
:   A subset of AI that enables systems to learn from data, identify patterns, and make decisions with minimal human intervention.

**Mobile Manipulator**
:   A robot system combining a mobile base (for locomotion) and a robotic arm (for manipulation), allowing for versatile interaction in dynamic environments.

**Motion Planning**
:   The process of finding a sequence of valid configurations that moves a robot from a start configuration to a target configuration while avoiding obstacles.

**MoveIt 2**
:   The most widely used software for mobile manipulation and inverse kinematics in ROS 2, providing powerful tools for motion planning, control, and 3D perception.

**Natural Language Understanding (NLU)**
:   A subtopic of natural language processing (NLP) that deals with machine reading comprehension. NLU helps computers understand and interpret human language.

**NVIDIA Omniverse**
:   An open platform built for virtual collaboration and real-time physically accurate simulation.

**Observation Space**
:   The set of all possible observations an agent can make about its environment through its sensors.

**Perception Module**
:   A software component in a robot's architecture responsible for processing sensor data to understand the environment, detect objects, and estimate their states.

**Physical AI**
:   A field of artificial intelligence focused on intelligent agents that interact with and learn from the physical world, often embodied in robots.

**PID Control (Proportional-Integral-Derivative)**
:   A widely used feedback control loop mechanism. A PID controller continuously calculates an error value as the difference between a desired setpoint and a measured process variable and applies a correction based on proportional, integral, and derivative terms.

**Proprioception**
:   The sense of the relative position of one's own body parts and strength of effort being employed in movement. In robotics, this is typically provided by joint encoders.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
:   A type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by performing actions in an environment to maximize a cumulative reward.

**Rigid Body Dynamics**
:   The branch of classical mechanics that studies the movement of systems of interconnected bodies under the action of external forces and torques. Essential for realistic robot simulation.

**Robot Operating System 2 (ROS 2)**
:   An open-source, meta-operating system for robots. It provides a flexible framework for writing robot software, offering tools, libraries, and conventions for building complex robotic systems.

**Self-Localization**
:   The ability of a robot to determine its own position and orientation within its environment.

**Sensor**
:   A device that detects and responds to some type of input from the physical environment. In robotics, sensors provide data about the robot's internal state and external surroundings.

**Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)**
:   The computational problem of constructing or updating a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously keeping track of an agent's location within it.

**Skeletal Hierarchy (Rig)**
:   A structured arrangement of bones/joints that defines the kinematic relationships of a 3D model, allowing it to be animated or controlled.

**State Estimation**
:   The process of inferring the current state (e.g., position, velocity, orientation) of a system based on noisy sensor measurements.

**Teleoperation**
:   The operation of a machine or robot from a distance.

**Torque Sensor**
:   A sensor that measures the rotational force (torque) applied to it. Used in robotics for force control and safe human-robot interaction.

**URDF (Unified Robot Description Format)**
:   An XML file format for describing all aspects of a robot. It is commonly used in ROS and Gazebo to define robot kinematics, dynamics, visual appearance, and collision properties.

**Unity**
:   A cross-platform game engine and development platform, widely used for creating 3D simulations, games, and interactive experiences, including high-fidelity robotics simulations.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA)**
:   An emerging field in AI that integrates computer vision, natural language understanding, and robotic action to enable robots to understand high-level commands and perform complex tasks in the real world.

**Virtual Reality (VR)**
:   A simulated experience that can be similar to or completely different from the real world.

**Waypoint Navigation**
:   A method of navigation where a robot moves from one predefined point (waypoint) to another in a sequence to reach a destination.
